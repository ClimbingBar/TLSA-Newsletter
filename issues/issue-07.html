EDITOR'S NOTE

Legal AI is connecting the work.

This week, legal AI became more connected to legal sources, deal documents, firm knowledge, and lawyer training. The useful question is what those connections let a lawyer inspect and verify. Being able to explain why a result is sound matters more than simply generating one.

ISSUE 07

Five articles from this week.

01

LEGAL RESEARCH

Harvey mapped the connections behind legal research.

Harvey says its new Graph approach maps relationships among authorities, citations, matter documents, parties, and versions so agents can retrieve and check connected sources.

Why it matters: Better legal AI depends on traceable context, not just persuasive prose. Lawyers still need to verify the authorities and reasoning.

Read Harvey's explanation ↗
02

TAX RESEARCH

Blue J tax research became available inside Legora.

Eligible customers can query Blue J from Legora and receive answers with inline links to the underlying sources.

Why it matters: Specialized research tools are moving into broader legal-work platforms. Source-linked answers help, but tax advice still requires checking the authority itself.

Read the integration announcement ↗
03

TRANSACTIONS

Ansarada and Legora connected data rooms to AI-powered deal work.

The beta integration lets permitted users bring data-room documents into Legora for diligence, drafting, and closing checklists, preserving existing access permissions and recording imports in the data room's audit trail.

Why it matters: In M&A, confidentiality and access control matter alongside analysis. Lawyers need to know what documents an AI tool is allowed to see.

Read the beta announcement ↗
04

LEGAL EDUCATION

NUS Law joined Legora's Legal AI Scholars Program.

Faculty and students will receive platform access and training for research, document review, and drafting. NUS emphasizes foundational legal skills and critical evaluation before introducing the tool.

Why it matters: Legal fundamentals help students decide when an AI output deserves trust, revision, or rejection. Access alone is not preparation for practice.

Read the education partnership ↗
05

FIRM ADOPTION

Jones Day selected Legora for a global rollout.

The firm plans to give lawyers across its global offices access over the coming months, help shape a future legal-research product, and develop a structured AI learning program.

Why it matters: Major firms are treating AI adoption as a training, governance, and client-service issue, not simply a software purchase.

Read the Jones Day announcement ↗
WHAT TO WATCH

Connections need controls.

Watch whether citation checking and audit trails become standard in AI-assisted work, how legal teams manage permissions across connected systems, and how law schools teach AI through supervised legal reasoning.

Would you rather learn AI by getting an answer faster, or by learning how to prove whether the answer is right?
