EDITOR'S NOTE

Legal AI is getting closer to the record.

This week’s updates point to the same shift: legal AI is moving into the systems where legal work, firm knowledge, and regulated decisions already live. The core skill remains the same: understand what the tool used, then check whether it supports the conclusion.

ISSUE 09

Five articles from this week.

01

AI GOVERNANCE

New York opened the RAISE Act compliance portal.

New York opened registration for large frontier-AI developers ahead of January 1, when safety frameworks, model-transparency reports, and critical-incident reporting requirements take effect.

Why it matters: AI law increasingly means compliance systems, documentation, and incident response, not just high-level policy.

Read the New York announcement ↗
02

REGULATION

California began gathering input on AI safeguards in insurance.

The California Department of Insurance launched an industry survey and set a December 15 public workshop on transparency, privacy, fairness, human oversight, and vendor accountability. No new AI regulation has been adopted through this initiative yet.

Why it matters: Insurance is a major legal market where AI affects underwriting, claims, fraud detection, and consumer rights. Lawyers will be asked to turn governance principles into workable rules.

Read the Department’s release ↗
03

LEGAL WORKFLOW

Litera brought document comparison and firm search into Microsoft Copilot for Legal.

Litera says its Compare and Firm AI Search plugins are now available within Copilot, pairing deterministic redlines with cited, permissions-aware firm-knowledge search.

Why it matters: Legal AI workflows combine different technologies. Know which part is probabilistic generation and which part is a rule-based comparison or verified retrieval step.

Read the Litera announcement ↗
04

FIRM ADOPTION

Addleshaw Goddard announced a firmwide Harvey collaboration.

Beginning in January, the firm plans to give Harvey to more than 3,400 lawyers and business-services professionals while co-developing workflows with its innovation group and clients.

Why it matters: Major firms are evaluating AI through real legal tasks, source checking, supervision, and client delivery, not simply generic productivity claims.

Read the collaboration announcement ↗
05

LEGAL RESEARCH

Harvey added Danish legislation and legislative history through Djøf Forlag.

The partnership will make licensed Danish statutes, executive orders, amendments, and preparatory works available in Harvey next year, with sources linked for subscribers.

Why it matters: The strongest legal-research AI tools connect answers to primary law and interpretive materials. Read the cited authority, not just the summary.

Read the research announcement ↗
WHAT TO WATCH

Source links and permissions are becoming basic features.

Watch how New York implements frontier-AI reporting, whether sector regulators turn workshops into specific rules, and whether legal AI platforms make source links, permissions, and human review standard rather than optional.
